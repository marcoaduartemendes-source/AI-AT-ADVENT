# Russia–Ukraine War: Scenario Assessment, Probabilities and Timelines

**Date of assessment:** 27 July 2026
**Method:** Independent analysis built from verifiable mid-2026 facts (battlefield geometry, attrition arithmetic, fiscal data, diplomatic record), not from any single outlet's narrative. Probabilities are subjective Bayesian estimates, time-bound and conditional, with explicit signposts that would move them.

---

## 1. Epistemic ground rules (how the facts were weighed)

Every number in the public record on this war is produced by a party with an interest. Before reasoning from "facts," each class of claim was discounted:

- **Territorial change** is the most verifiable data class (satellite + geolocated footage, triangulated by ISW, DeepState, ACLED). Independent trackers put net Russian gains at roughly **5–15 square miles per four-week period** in mid-2026 — the two ends of that range come from different methodologies, and I treat the truth as inside it. Either way it is a near-total stall versus 2024–25 tempo.
- **Russian casualty figures** cited in Western media largely originate from Ukraine's General Staff and are systematically high. Even discounted by 30–40%, the consolidated Western-agency estimate of **~197k Russian casualties in H1 2026** leaves the core claim intact: Russian daily losses (~1,000–1,500) now **exceed daily contract recruitment (~940 vs. a 1,100–1,150 target)** — the first sustained inversion of the war. The direction of this inversion matters more than its exact magnitude.
- **Ukraine's claim that 42.7% of Russian designed refining capacity is disabled** is a maximum figure (source: Ukraine's General Staff). Refineries are partially repairable; effective sustained loss is plausibly **20–30%**. But the independent corroboration is strong: fuel shortages reported as far away as Siberia, IEA projections of depressed refinery runs, ~194 confirmed refinery strikes in H1 2026 (11× the H1 2025 rate), and hits on all 11 of Russia's largest refineries, including Omsk and Tyumen at ~2,000–2,500 km depth.
- **Russian fiscal data** is the hardest to fake in aggregate: oil-and-gas budget revenue **down ~45% year-on-year in Q1 2026**, GDP growth projected ~0.4%, a widening deficit financed by drawing down reserves. Kiel Institute's judgment that the binding constraint is now "people, technology and productive capacity, not money" is consistent with everything else observed.
- **Opinion polling in both countries** (64% of Russians favor negotiations; 66% of Ukrainians want their government to seek a negotiated end) is directionally credible but does not translate into policy in either system — Russia because preferences don't constrain the Kremlin, Ukraine because "negotiate" polls very differently from "cede Donbas."

One widely-circulated claim was **excluded as unreliable**: reports of a "coup threat" against Zelensky sourced to low-credibility outlets. The verifiable core of Ukraine's political crisis is serious enough without embellishment (see §2.4).

## 2. The load-bearing facts, mid-2026

### 2.1 The battlefield has inverted in character
Russia's 2026 spring offensive failed. The summer campaign's main objective — **Kostiantynivka**, assaulted by two tactical groupings — has not fallen despite Moscow's 3 July claim; Russian pressure around Pokrovsk continues without operational breakthrough. Cost-per-gain has exploded: Ukrainian-side estimates of **~316 Russian casualties per km² captured in 2026, versus ~120 in 2025**. Even halved, that is an unpayable exchange rate for a state that is simultaneously losing its recruitment race.

The deeper point: **the drone-saturated battlefield has made the defense dominant at the tactical level in both directions.** Neither army can mass for a breakthrough without being attrited before contact. This is not a temporary condition — it is the war's new physics, and it structurally caps what either side can win by force.

### 2.2 The war's center of gravity moved off the front line
Ukraine's theory of victory quietly changed in 2025–26 from "liberate territory" to "strangle the Russian war economy from the air." The refinery campaign, strikes on shadow-fleet vessels in the Azov corridor, and US secondary sanctions on buyers of Russian oil/uranium/gas now compound: Russia is fighting a land war it cannot win fast, funded by an energy economy being disassembled faster than it can be repaired. Russia's countervalue answer — mass air strikes on Kyiv and the energy grid — imposes pain but has never converted into Ukrainian political concession in four years of trying.

### 2.3 Both sides' clocks are running, but they measure different things
- **Russia's clock is economic-industrial:** reserve depletion, 0.4% growth, refinery attrition, and a recruitment shortfall (~195k contracts signed against a 409k annual target, ~5% behind even the interim pace). Elite tension is reportedly rising (OSW, May 2026) and the regime has no succession mechanism — but repression is effective and there is no visible fracture. Russia can plausibly sustain *this reduced intensity* of war into 2027–28; it cannot regain 2024's offensive tempo.
- **Ukraine's clock is demographic-political:** manpower remains the binding constraint (80–100k trained personnel needed in the pipeline to hold current lines), and the **energy-sector corruption scandal is now the gravest internal threat of the war** — Yermak forced out, three ministers gone, NABU charges expanding into defense and banking, and for the first time allegations that touch Zelensky personally, alongside US pressure to hold elections. European money is not the constraint: the April 2026 **$104B loan package (~$70B military)** covers 2026–27.
- **Washington's clock is political:** the Trump administration has spent over a year on the Witkoff/Kushner track (the Nov-2025 28-point plan, Geneva counterproposals, Miami talks, two collapsed 2026 truces) and needs a deliverable before the November 2026 midterms.

### 2.4 The diplomatic record shows a pattern, not noise
Every 2026 negotiation round has died on the same two rocks: **(1) Russia demands all of Donbas including the ~25% Ukraine still holds — the "Fortress Belt" it has failed to take by force; (2) Ukraine demands legally binding Western security guarantees before any territorial discussion.** The two brief truces (32-hour Easter, 9–11 May) held approximately, then lapsed — proving a ceasefire is *mechanically* possible and *politically* unanchored. The current live initiative (as of 26 July 2026) is deliberately smaller: a **mutual halt to air strikes**, prepared by Kyiv and Washington for presentation to Moscow, with Witkoff/Kushner expected in Moscow in early August.

## 3. The core analytical judgment

**Neither side retains a military theory of victory, but both retain a theory of not-losing — and those are asymmetric.**

Russia's theory of not-losing is *outlasting*: hold what it has, keep bleeding Ukraine, and wait for Kyiv's politics or Western cohesion to break. Ukraine's theory of not-losing is *strangulation plus survival*: make the war progressively unaffordable for Moscow while European money and its own defense industry keep the line manned.

This configuration almost never produces a clean peace. It produces **war termination by decay**: intensity ratchets down through partial, functional ceasefires (air first, then sea, then land) because each partial ceasefire is separately rational for both sides even while the political questions (Donbas sovereignty, NATO, sanctions) remain unresolved. The historical analog is not Minsk (a ceasefire imposed on a loser) but **Korea 1951–53**: two years of fighting *during* talks, ending in an armistice that settled nothing de jure and everything de facto.

The single most likely "final outcome" is therefore **an armistice line, internationally monitored, without a peace treaty, without recognition of annexations, and without Ukrainian NATO membership — but with Ukraine armed to the teeth and anchored westward.** The interesting questions are the timing, the path, and the tail risks. That is what the scenarios below price.

A second-order original point: **the air-ceasefire proposal is more strategically significant than it looks.** For Russia, accepting it means giving up its only remaining coercive lever against Ukrainian civil society (the strategic air campaign) while its ground offensive is stalled — effectively conceding the war's decision to the attrition ledger, which now runs against Moscow. For Ukraine, it means giving up the refinery campaign — its most effective instrument of the entire war. Whoever accepts first is signaling which clock they believe is shorter. Russia's evasive response ("premature to comment") while its refineries burn suggests Moscow knows its own answer.

## 4. Scenarios, probabilities, timelines

Probabilities are for the stated horizon and sum to 100% across mutually exclusive primary paths. Sub-branches are conditional.

### Scenario A — Armistice by accretion (the Korean path) · **45%**
**Path:** Partial air/energy ceasefire agreed in some form between **Q4 2026 and mid-2027** (first attempt Aug–Oct 2026 may fail and be revived; the US midterm window on 3 Nov 2026 is the forcing function on Washington's side, the 2027 budget cycle on Moscow's). Ground fighting continues at declining intensity for months alongside talks — Korea-style. A comprehensive line-of-contact ceasefire with a US-led monitoring mechanism and the European "reassurance force" (realistically 5,000–30,000 troops, not the advertised scale) follows within **6–18 months of the first durable partial ceasefire**, i.e. **most likely window: mid-2027 to mid-2028.**
**Terms that make it possible:** de facto (never de jure) freeze on the actual line; Donbas cession demand quietly dropped by Moscow or fudged (demilitarized status for the Fortress Belt rather than transfer); security guarantees delivered as bilateral Western commitments + tripwire force rather than NATO Article 5; sanctions relief phased and reversible.
**Sub-branch A′ (within the 45%):** the first armistice **collapses back into war within 3 years** — conditional probability ~35% — because guarantees under-deliver or a post-Putin Russia relitigates. A frozen conflict is an outcome, not an ending.

### Scenario B — Grinding continuation, no durable ceasefire through 2027 · **30%**
**Path:** The air-ceasefire initiative fails like its predecessors (Putin again prices Donbas above a deal; or a mass-casualty strike torpedoes talks, as in the Miami round). The war continues as it is now: a slow-motion positional fight with deepening mutual exhaustion, Russia gaining single-digit square miles a month at unsustainable cost, Ukraine holding but hollowing. Talks recur and fail on a ~quarterly rhythm.
**Why it's less likely than A:** the 2026 inversion is real — Russia is now losing men faster than it recruits, revenue faster than it adapts, and refining capacity faster than it repairs, for near-zero territorial return. Continuation is Putin's *revealed* preference but its price curve is steepening quarter over quarter. B mostly matters as the corridor through which A eventually arrives later (2028+) and worse.

### Scenario C — Ukrainian political rupture forces a bad deal · **10%**
**Path:** The corruption scandal metastasizes (credible evidence of personal enrichment by Zelensky, or a contested/botched wartime election under US pressure), mobilization legitimacy collapses, front-line manning fails faster than European money can compensate. A weakened or successor Ukrainian government accepts something close to Russia's terms — Donbas withdrawal, hard neutrality — to stop the war.
**Why only 10%:** Ukraine's institutions have processed four years of shocks; NABU acting against the president's circle is evidence of institutional function, not just rot; European funding is secured through 2027; and Ukrainian public opinion wants negotiation, not capitulation — the distinction that limits how bad a deal any survivable Kyiv government can sign. This is the scenario Russian strategy is *betting on*, which is precisely why Kyiv's domestic politics — not the front line — is the single most important variable to watch.

### Scenario D — Russian discontinuity accelerates termination · **8%**
**Path:** Fiscal crunch (reserve exhaustion meeting the 2027 budget), a fuel/logistics crisis from the refinery campaign, or an elite/succession event (Putin is 73; the system has no mechanism) forces Moscow to take the deal on the table roughly as-is, or triggers a contested transition during which the war effort degrades unilaterally.
**Why only 8%:** personalist regimes under sanctions historically grind on far past apparent breaking points; repression is holding; and elites reportedly remain consolidated precisely because the war leaves them no alternative. But this tail is *fatter than consensus assumes* — the May 2026 reporting on Putin's weakening position, rising elite tension, and a revenue collapse of this speed are the kind of pre-rupture signature that is only ever legible in hindsight. Note: a Putin exit does not guarantee peace — a hardline securocrat succession that escalates is inside this branch too.

### Scenario E — Major escalation (direct NATO–Russia clash / nuclear use) · **3%**
**Path:** Deep-strike spillover, a mass-casualty incident in a NATO state, escalation around the reassurance force's deployment, or nuclear signaling converted to use as the Kremlin's position deteriorates (the majority-Russian polling result "escalate if talks fail" is the permissive backdrop).
**Why 3% and not lower:** four years of steadily crossed "red lines" without nuclear use argue for a low number; but Scenario D's stressors and the physical entry of Western troops into Ukraine (Scenario A's endgame) both mechanically raise contact risk. Most escalation events would be contained; this 3% is for the uncontained branch.

### Scenario F — Comprehensive peace treaty (settled borders, lifted sanctions, normalized relations) · **2%**
Requires one side to accept juridical defeat. No mechanism visible on any horizon inside this decade. Included only for completeness; anyone selling this outcome is selling narrative.

### Long-horizon distribution (10-year, unconditional)
- Frozen/armistice line as the enduring status quo (Cyprus/Korea-type): **~55–60%**
- Renewed major war after a freeze: **~15–20%**
- De jure settlement eventually ratified (likely only post-Putin): **~8%**
- War still active in its current form in 2031: **~10%**
- Escalation/other: **~5%**

## 5. Timeline of decision points (signposts to watch)

| Window | Event | What it discriminates |
|---|---|---|
| **Aug–Sep 2026** | Witkoff/Kushner Moscow trip; Russian answer on the air ceasefire | Acceptance → A jumps toward 55%+; rejection + new mass strikes → B strengthens |
| **Sep–Oct 2026** | Russian summer offensive culminates (Kostiantynivka verdict) | If the Fortress Belt holds through October, Russia's last plausible territorial argument for 2026 dies → raises A |
| **3 Nov 2026** | US midterms | Pre-midterm deal pressure peaks before it; after it, Washington's urgency partially resets |
| **Q4 2026** | Russian 2027 budget publication | Deficit financing choices reveal how short Moscow's real fiscal runway is (watch NWF drawdown and hidden defense lines) |
| **Winter 2026–27** | Strike-campaign reciprocity (grid vs. refineries) | If both campaigns run full-rate through winter, the air-ceasefire logic revives in spring from a harder starting point |
| **H1 2027** | Ukrainian election question resolved (held, postponed with consensus, or contested) | Contested → C risk doubles; consensus path → C fades |
| **Continuous** | NABU/SAPO trajectory vs. Zelensky's inner circle; Russian monthly contract-recruitment numbers; refinery repair rate | The three highest-information time series of the war |

## 6. What would change this assessment

- **Russia accepts the air ceasefire quickly** → the "shorter clock" hypothesis about Moscow confirmed; shift 10+ points from B to A and pull A's timeline in by ~2 quarters.
- **Kostiantynivka falls before October** → Russian coercive leverage partially restored; shift ~5–8 points from A to B; Donbas-cession demand hardens.
- **Verified evidence of Zelensky's personal corruption, or a rupture between Kyiv and NABU** → double C to ~20% at the expense of A.
- **Russian recruitment recovers above ~1,200/day for two consecutive quarters** → the attrition-inversion thesis breaks; rebalance toward B and lengthen all timelines by a year.
- **A NATO-territory mass-casualty event** → re-price E immediately.

---

## 7. Bottom line

This war no longer has a military ending available to either side; it has only political endings, and the politics are maturing asymmetrically — faster in Washington and Kyiv than in Moscow, but with Moscow's economic floor now visibly cracking. The modal outcome is **not a peace deal but an armistice assembled in pieces** — air, sea, then land — most likely first cemented **between late 2026 and mid-2027**, hardening into a monitored frozen line by **2027–28**, with the political questions (annexations, NATO, sanctions) deliberately left unresolved for a decade or more. The war's true "final outcome" — whether the frozen line becomes a stable Korea or a reloading Minsk — will be decided less by the treaty text than by two things: how heavily Ukraine is armed the day the shooting stops, and who rules Russia the day Putin doesn't.
